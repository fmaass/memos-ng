memosng — privacy

Last updated 24 August 2026

I operate this service for my household. The memos-ng server runs on hardware I own, at memos.discomarder.live, and the memosng app on your phone talks to that server and to nothing else.

What the app keeps on your phone

  • The sign-in token, in the device keychain. It is removed when you sign out, and when the server rejects it.
  • Captures that have not been uploaded yet — your text and a copy of each photo or video — in the app's own storage, until the server has them.
  • Recently viewed pictures, cached so the timeline does not fetch the same photo twice. The copies held in memory are dropped when you sign out or switch accounts. The copies on disk were fetched through links that expire, and an entry whose link has expired is discarded rather than shown again.

What the server keeps

  • Your account, as the household sign-on knows it.
  • Your memos: text, dates, tags, and the visibility you chose.
  • Photos and videos as you uploaded them — unchanged originals, including whatever metadata the camera wrote into them — plus the smaller versions the server derives for the timeline and for full-screen viewing.
  • Tokens issued to your devices, and browser sessions.
  • Technical logs of the requests it serves, as any web server does.

Who can see a memo

Every memo carries one of three settings, and you pick it when you post:

  • Private — only you.
  • Protected — anyone signed in to this server, which means the rest of the household.
  • Public — anyone who can reach the server address, with no sign-in at all. Public memos and their pictures are served to visitors who have not signed in; that is what the setting means here, not a leak. Most memos in this household are posted Public, so check the setting before posting something you would rather keep in.

What does not happen

  • No analytics, no tracking, no advertising identifier, no ads.
  • No third-party service receives your memos, your photos or your account. The app's only network destinations are the household server and, while you sign in, the household sign-on page opened in the system browser. The app never sees your password.
  • Nothing is sold, and nothing is handed to anyone outside the household.
  • No cloud storage other than the household server.
  • The app asks for no location.

While the app is in TestFlight

Apple distributes the beta builds and gives me the crash reports from them, together with any feedback a tester chooses to send through TestFlight. That channel is Apple's, covered by Apple's own privacy terms; I add no reporting of my own on top of it.

Deleting things

Deleting a memo in the app removes it from the server: it leaves the timeline, and its pictures stop being served. If you want something gone from the server's storage for good, including copies in any backup I hold, ask me and I will remove it.

Signing out takes the token off the phone and revokes it on the server, so a device that is signed out cannot read anything.

Children

This is a family app, run by the family for itself. Accounts exist only because I created them for people in my household. There is no sign-up, and nobody outside the household can get one.

Asking me something

I administer this server myself, and I am the only person who does. If you have a question about your data — what is stored, or getting it removed — ask me directly. Everyone with an account here knows how to reach me.

Changes

If what the app or the server does with your data changes, I change this page and date it.
